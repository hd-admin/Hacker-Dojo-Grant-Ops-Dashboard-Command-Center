# Hacker Dojo — Peer Org & Funder Research

Research compiled for the Hacker Dojo Grant Ops Dashboard / Command Center. All funder data comes from sources actually loaded during this research session (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Wikipedia, organiztion websites, and news articles). No data was inferred or invented.

## 1. Hacker Dojo — Base Profile

**Legal name:** Hacker Dojo
**EIN:** 26-4812213
**Address:** 855 Maude Ave, Mountain View, CA 94043 (per hackerdojo.org). Earlier address: 3350 Thomas Rd, Santa Clara, CA (2019 press).
**IRS status:** 501(c)(3), tax-exempt since July 2012. NTEE category: Educational Institutions and Related Activities / Educational Services and Schools – Other.
**Year founded:** 2009 (inc. nonprofit 2010; IRS 501(c)(3) recognized 2012)
**Mission (per website):** "A collaborative hackerspace where tech enthusiasts gather to build, experiment and improve." Programs include 24/7 coworking, hardware/maker labs, classes/workshops/hackathons, the new "AI Career Initiative" (AI Stars, Summer Camp, Accelerator).
**Notable alumni/affiliates (per website + press):** Pinterest (early), Pebble Watch (Eric Migocovsky — quoted on homepage), Steve Wozniak (quoted endorser), Word Lens (acquired by Google → Google Translate), Lambda School founder Austin Allred.
**Sources loaded:** hackerdojo.org (homepage), ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer summary page, svvoice.com 2019 anniversary article, NYT 2012 "Techies Fight to Save Hacker Dojo".

**Key interpretation:** The "Contributions" line on Hacker Dojo's 990s (the dedicated grants/donations line) is modest — typically $7k–$100k/year. The bulk of revenue ($400k–$700k+) comes from "Program Services," i.e., membership fees, event fees, and room rental. This is typical of a hackerspace — most peer orgs in this research set show the same pattern. **Hacker Dojo is therefore primarily membership-funded, with institutional grants as a secondary and underdeveloped revenue line.** That is the strategic gap the Grant Ops Dashboard is presumably designed to close.

### Hacker Dojo — known funders found in this research

**Confidence: HIGH (named on Hacker Dojo's own surfaces):**
- **Member dues** — Standard $150/mo, Student $75, Veteran $135, Dedicated Desk +$225/mo (per hackerdojo.org/pricing).
- **Every.org** — Hacker Dojo's official donation platform ("Donate" button on hackerdojo.org → every.org/hacker-dojo). Every.org aggregates donor-advised fund gifts and individual giving; this is a giving infrastructure, not a funder itself.
- **Kickstarter (crowdfunding)** — Referenced in 2012 NYT article: a Kickstarter campaign ran to save Hacker Dojo when threatened with eviction by Mountain View. Specific backers are not listed in the source loaded.

**Confidence: MEDIUM (public press, not a 990 schedule):**
- **Pebble Watch team / Eric Migicovsky** — Strongly tied to Hacker Dojo (quoted on homepage, listed in NYT 2012 article as past "West Coast HQ"). No documented cash grant, but in-kind/donation relationship almost certain.
- **Steve Wozniak** — Quoted endorser on hackerdojo.org homepage. Quote: "I want to share a good cause – a non-profit hacker space community that supports what I hold near and dear." No documented grant amount.

**Confidence: LOW (no Schedule B detail available in this research):**
- The 990s ProPublica links for Hacker Dojo list Schedule B as attached in some years, but ProPublica's full-filing viewer renders Schedule B via JavaScript, which is not loadable in this research session. **The actual list of donors ≥$5,000 from Hacker Dojo's own 990 Schedule Bs is not in this dataset.** That is a known gap; if more detail is needed, the Schedule B PDFs must be downloaded directly from ProPublica (URL pattern: `https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/264812213/...`) or from the IRS.

**Strategic note:** Hacker Dojo's nonprofit filings show no obvious large institutional funder. The 2012 NYT article describes Hacker Dojo scrambling to do an underwear charity run and a Kickstarter rather than tapping foundation grants — confirming that institutional fundraising has historically been weak at Hacker Dojo specifically, even though peer orgs in the same size bracket have built substantial foundation/fundraising programs (see Open Works, Artisan's Asylum below).

## 4. Patterns and Strategic Observations for Hacker Dojo

1. **The hackerspace peer set is bifurcated.** Org 990s show two clusters:
   - **Member-funded small orgs** (HacDC, Noisebridge, NYC Resistor, Sudo Room, Ace Monster Toys) — contributions are small dollar amounts from individuals; no large foundation grants visible in 990 line items.
   - **Foundation- and government-funded larger orgs** (Open Works, Artisan's Asylum, Maker Ed 2018–2021) — these have built programs that fit standard RFPs (workforce development, youth STEM, small-business support, manufacturing retention).
   - **Hacker Dojo currently sits in the first cluster** despite the new AI Career Initiative being a natural fit for the second cluster's funding streams.

2. **Geography is decisive for Hacker Dojo.** The strongest peer-funder match is **Open Works in Baltimore**, but Baltimore funders don't fund Mountain View. The next-best match is **Maker Nexus in Sunnyvale** (~5 miles away, same labor market, same funder ecosystem) — but Maker Nexus' own funder list is not visible in this research. **Hacker Dojo's realistic funder pool is Bay Area + California + national foundations that fund tech-community / workforce / maker education programs.**

3. **The peer that most clearly demonstrates the model Hacker Dojo should emulate is Open Works:**
   - Same basic org size and shape (community workshop, small staff, member revenue)
   - Same kind of mission (community-driven making with educational/economic-development overlay)
   - But Open Works has built a $1.2M/year grants program with 50+ named institutional funders
   - Many of Open Works' funders are Baltimore-specific and won't apply directly to Mountain View, but the *type* of funder (city DHCDs, state arts councils, state economic development agencies, family foundations, corporate foundations) absolutely exists in Silicon Valley

4. **Likely Bay Area funders to research next** (not confirmed in this research but high-probability given peer patterns):
   - **Silicon Valley Community Foundation** (largest community foundation in region)
   - **Sobrato Family Foundation**, **David and Lucile Packard Foundation**, **Hewlett Foundation** (large Bay Area foundations with tech/workforce portfolios)
   - **Knight Foundation** (national, funds "informed and engaged communities" including civic tech spaces)
   - **Kapor Foundation** (Oakland, explicitly focused on tech diversity/inclusion — adjacent to Hacker Dojo's AI Career Initiative)
   - **Google.org**, **Microsoft Philanthropies**, **Salesforce.org** (tech corporate foundations with workforce development programs)
   - **City of Mountain View**, **Santa Clara County**, **California Workforce Development Board**
   - **Mozilla Foundation** (internet health / open web / digital literacy — strong mission alignment)

5. **Candid/GuideStar profiles were not consulted in this research session.** Those would give richer funder-network data. Suggest running the EINs above through `candid.org` for the next pass.

6. **Schedule B data is a known gap.** ProPublica's full-filing viewer uses JavaScript to render Schedule B; the static fetch used in this research cannot extract individual donor names. For a complete funder list, the underlying XML / PDF files need to be downloaded and parsed directly. URLs follow the pattern:
   - `https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/download-xml?object_id=<OBJECT_ID>`
   - `https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/<EIN>/<OBJECT_ID>/download990pdf_<...>_prefixes_<...>.pdf`
   Specific object_ids for Hacker Dojo's Schedule B-containing years (2014, 2019, 2022, 2023) would need to be pulled from the filing index pages.

## 6. Suggested next research steps

1. **Download and parse IRS Schedule B PDFs/XML for**: Hacker Dojo, Artisan's Asylum, Open Works, Maker Nexus, Maker Ed. This is the single highest-value gap.
2. **Run the same EINs through candid.org / GuideStar** for funder-network analytics, especially for Open Works and Maker Ed.
3. **Search California Secretary of State bizfile** for any "Hacker Dojo Foundation" or related 501(c)(3) entities that may have been created for fundraising.
4. **Check Mountain View and Santa Clara County grant programs** (City of MV community grants, Santa Clara County Office of Sustainability, etc.) — most likely untested local funding.
5. **Search 990 Schedule I (grant-making)** for Hacker Dojo — if any org has given grants *to* Hacker Dojo, that appears on the *grantor's* 990, not Hacker Dojo's. Search by HN name in ProPublica full-text search.
6. **Look at Trellis (formerly Foundation Center) 990 finder / Foundation Directory** for funders that have given to "hackerspace" or "makerspace" keywords.
